Research & Project Annotations

Akenji, Lewis, et al. "Circular Economy and Plastics." Circular Economy and Plastics: A Gap-Analysis in ASEAN Member States, Institute for Global Environmental Strategies, 2019, pp. 14–20. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/resrep21872.6. Accessed 24 Mar. 2026.
This source is credible because it is a part of a book on the JSTOR website, which is a website well know for its credibility and the experts who write for it. So far, I have learned about the idea of a circular economy, which recycling plays a key role in. Essentially, a circular economy involves reusing items instead of discarding them after a single use, allowing them to offset the creation of new materials. I plan to use this source to explain why recycling is important and why citizens should be actively taking the necessary steps to have their items properly recycled.
Burcea, Ştefan Gabriel. "The Economical, Social and Environmental Implications of Informal Waste Collection and Recycling." Theoretical and Empirical Researches in Urban Management, vol. 10, no. 3, 2015, pp. 14–24. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/24873532. Accessed 27 Mar. 2026.
The main topic of the article is the difference between informal and formal recycling. Formal recycling is done by enterprises and is much more efficient. I would like to inform people about how important it is for companies and larger groups to ensure the waste they create is dealt with responsibly. For example, a factory might make things from plastic in molds, but each piece will have some waste.
Carey, John. "On the Brink of a Recycling Revolution?" Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, vol. 114, no. 4, 2017, pp. 612–16. JSTOR, https://www.jstor.org/stable/26479025. Accessed 27 Mar. 2026.
I learned that the MBA director Mike Biddle says that they are going through a “quiet revolution” in the technology of product sorting. Saint Louis could take some of this new technology to increase the amount of contamination allowed in bins. The current problem is people putting trash into recycling bins; if new technology could filter it out, we could simplify our recycling process significantly.
"City Dumpsters and Rollcarts - Saint Louis City Recycles." Saint Louis City Recycles, 9 Mar. 2026, stlcityrecycles.com. Accessed 29 Mar. 2026.
This source is the official recycling website for Saint Louis. I would like to use this to inform people about how they should recycle to ensure their efforts do not go to waste. A key insight: "The City of St. Louis has shifted from alley recycling to drop-off recycling due to ineffectiveness and high costs." This change is the highlight of our advocacy project.

Our main plan is to use this source to explain why Saint Louis no longer has an alleyway recycling system, diving into specific stats. We also want to use clips in our video to add credibility.

Spencer, Cara. Personal Interview. 5 April 2026.
