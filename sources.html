Recycle St. Louis Project

🎤 Primary Source: Interview with Mayor Cara Spencer
Speaker 1 (00:02): Can you introduce yourself really quick?
Speaker 2 (00:09): Sure. My name is Cara Spencer, and I'm the mayor of the city of St. Louis.
Speaker 1 (00:14): Do you see the current dropoff system as a permanent or temporary solution to a recycling situation?
Speaker 2 (00:29): ...We were one of the few cities in the nation that had alley recycling... the biggest issue that we've seen is that the waste in the blue bins was remarkably contaminated... the overwhelming majority of material wasn't actually getting recycled... if your neighbors were throwing garbage in that blue dumpster, it was all going to a landfill.
Speaker 2 (03:16): Believe it or not, we don't actually pay for recycling or trash removal with the fees we pay... every household pays less than 20 bucks a month. It doesn't actually cover the cost.
Speaker 2 (11:48): One of the biggest ways in which young people can improve what goes into landfills is by consuming less, buy stuff secondhand and look for ways in which you can prevent things from being made to begin with.

Akenji, Lewis, et al. "Circular Economy and Plastics." Circular Economy and Plastics: A Gap-Analysis in ASEAN Member States, Institute for Global Environmental Strategies, 2019, pp. 14–20. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/resrep21872.6. Accessed 24 Mar. 2026.
A circular economy involves reusing items instead of discarding them after a single use. I plan to use this to explain the importance of resource conservation.
Burcea, Ştefan Gabriel. "The Economical, Social and Environmental Implications of Informal Waste Collection and Recycling." Theoretical and Empirical Researches in Urban Management, vol. 10, no. 3, 2015, pp. 14–24. JSTOR, http://www.jstor.org/stable/24873532. Accessed 27 Mar. 2026.
Focuses on the difference between formal and informal recycling. I want to highlight the responsibility of large factories to deal with manufacturing waste.
Carey, John. "On the Brink of a Recycling Revolution?" PNAS, vol. 114, no. 4, 2017, pp. 612–16. JSTOR, https://www.jstor.org/stable/26479025. Accessed 27 Mar. 2026.
Discusses the "quiet revolution" in sorting technology. Suggests St. Louis could adopt innovations to handle bin contamination.
"City Dumpsters and Rollcarts - Saint Louis City Recycles." Saint Louis City Recycles, 9 Mar. 2026, stlcityrecycles.com. Accessed 29 Mar. 2026.
Official city resource. Confirms the shift from alley recycling to drop-off due to high costs and ineffectiveness.
Cityofstlouismissouri. "Public Infrastructure & Utilities Committee – August 14, 2025." YouTube, 14 Aug. 2025, www.youtube.com/watch?v=crYaIlizSxU.
Official filming of a city meeting. Provides helpful stats on the percentage of waste that actually gets recycled.
Echeverría, Daniella, et al. "Waste Recycling and Reuse." International Institute for Sustainable Development, 2020, pp. 7–8. JSTOR. Accessed 24 Mar. 2026.
Explains why recycling is difficult on a mass scale and emphasizes that citizen involvement is non-negotiable for success.
Ghosh, Sanchari. "Electronic Waste Recycling for Developing Economies." Economic and Political Weekly, vol. 46, no. 49, 2011, pp. 17–21. JSTOR. Accessed 27 Mar. 2026.
Highlights that supporting recycling in growing economies is vital as they create significant portions of global waste.
Goodship, Vannessa. "Plastic Recycling." Science Progress, vol. 90, no. 4, 2007, pp. 245–68. JSTOR. Accessed 29 Mar. 2026.
Provides technical info on polymers and additives. Explains how chemical makeup determines if a plastic can actually be melted and reused.
Jain, Akshat, editor. "What Can Be Recycled and How Many Times?" Plastic Recycling: Decoded, 2021, pp. 18–22. JSTOR. Accessed 24 Mar. 2026.
Details the RIC code system and "down-cycling"—the process where materials lose quality each time they are processed.
Krull, Ryan. "St. Louis Officials Say Recycling Revamp Is Already Paying Off." St. Louis Magazine, 29 Oct. 2025, www.stlmag.com/news/stlouis-recycling-drop-sites-results/.
Local coverage of the drop-site shift. Contains quotes from Cara Spencer that we used to prepare for our interview.
Spencer, Cara. Personal Interview. 5 April 2026.
Primary interview source. Discussed bottlenecks like funding, contamination, and the possibility of "opt-in" roll-cart programs.
United States Congress. Save Our Seas 2.0 Act. S. 1982, 116th Congress, 2019–2020. Congress.gov.
Federal law aimed at innovation. We analyzed its shortcomings to advocate for more aggressive local recycling policies.
United States EPA. "National Recycling Strategy." EPA.gov, 13 Nov. 2019. Accessed 23 Jan. 2026.
Outlines the goal to increase US recycling to 50%. We find some of these goals unambitious compared to the scale of the waste problem.
United States EPA. "What Is a Circular Economy?" EPA.gov, 4 Sept. 2025.
Explains the transition from a linear "take-make-waste" model to a circular one. Helpful graphics for explaining the lifecycle of materials.
van Ewijk, Stijn, and Julia Stegemann. "Waste Recycling." An Introduction to Waste Management, UCL Press, 2023. JSTOR. Accessed 23 Mar. 2026.
Details "open" vs "closed" loop recycling. Emphasizes that recycling is never perfect due to unavoidable material loss.
"Waste and Recycling Studies and Reports." Missouri DNR, 2020, dnr.mo.gov.
State-level studies. Includes a fascinating report on using recycled tires to replace limestone in concrete for sound insulation.
